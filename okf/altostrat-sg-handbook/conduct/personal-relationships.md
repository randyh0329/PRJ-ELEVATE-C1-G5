# General expectations

Colleagues and extended workforce members become friends, but boundaries apply to
romantic, physical and familial relationships. In all such relationships you must comply
with the Code of Conduct, the value of respecting each other, and all policies against
harassment, discrimination and retaliation. Non-compliance may lead to discipline up to
and including termination.[^hb-8]

# Prohibited supervisory relationships

Romantic or physical relationships are **strictly prohibited** where one individual is in
a position to **supervise or exercise authority** over the other.[^hb-8]

The prohibition reaches beyond the org chart. It applies to:[^hb-8]

* All reporting lines, **including dotted-line reporting**
* **Cross-functional project teams**
* **Technical Lead roles** where one individual exercises supervision or authority, even
  with no direct reporting relationship

# Familial relationships

Subject to **exactly the same restrictions** as romantic and physical
relationships.[^hb-8]

**Definition.** A familial relationship includes a child, parent, in-law, grandparent,
grandchild, sibling, aunt, uncle, cousin, nephew, niece, **and anyone else whose
relationship to you could impair - or be perceived as impairing - your objective
judgement**.[^hb-8] The final clause makes the list open-ended, not exhaustive.

# Active job candidates

If you are in a romantic or physical relationship with an active job candidate you
must:[^hb-8]

1. Not participate in the hiring process, and **promptly recuse yourself**.
2. If you supervise the role applied for, **inform HR** so the candidate is not hired
   into that specific role.

The summary layer adds that you must **promptly disclose the relationship to
HR**.[^hb-5-3]

# Permitted relationships and conflict thresholds

Relationships between employees or extended workforce members are **generally permitted**
provided they are consensual, do not negatively affect the work environment, and create no
actual or perceived conflict of interest.[^hb-8]

**A conflict exists if** there are issues of unfairness, favouritism or harassment, **or**
if one person would be expected, as part of their job, to access the other's **salary
information, performance reviews or other confidential information**.[^hb-8]

**Higher-risk scenarios:** working on the same project, or reporting to the same
manager.[^hb-8]

# Mandatory reporting

* **Employees:** a relationship or team structure that is not permitted, creates a
  conflict, or causes negative workplace impact must be reported **immediately** to HR
  and/or your manager. Failure to report promptly may result in discipline up to and
  including termination.[^hb-8] The summary layer states that **both parties and their
  managers** must report.[^hb-5-3]
* **Managers:** a manager aware of a prohibited relationship, or one causing conflict or
  negative impact, must report it promptly to HR - **regardless of whether it falls within
  their reporting line**.[^hb-8]

# VP and executive disclosure mandate

All employees at **VP level or above** must disclose **any** romantic, physical or
familial relationship with anyone they know to be an employee, extended workforce member
or active job candidate - **regardless of reporting line or managerial
authority**.[^hb-8]

This data is shared with the **Head of People Operations** and the **Chief Legal
Officer**, and reported **on an aggregate basis to the Board of Directors**.[^hb-8]

# Remediation

Where a relationship is prohibited, or HR or management assess that it creates a conflict
of interest or negative workplace impact, Altostrat will act. Remediation may include
**transfer, reassignment, ending employment, or terminating a business contract** - for
one or both individuals.[^hb-8]
