# Applicability

* **Applies to:** company employees **only**.[^hb-17]
* **Does not apply to:** temporary staff, vendors or contractors.[^hb-17]
* **Status:** this procedure **does not form part of your contract of employment** and
  **may be amended at any time**.[^hb-17]

# What counts as a flexible working request

A request to do any or all of:[^hb-17]

* Reduce or vary your **working hours**
* Reduce or vary the **days** you work
* Work from a **different location**, for example from home
* Any **combination** of the above

# Process

| Step | Requirement |
|------|-------------|
| **Eligibility** | You must be a current employee **at the date of your request**[^hb-17] |
| **Submission** | **Formally in writing**, by email to your Manager **or** by opening an HR case in ITSM[^hb-17] |
| **Decision** | Altostrat evaluates and informs you of the decision **within 2 months**[^hb-17] |

# Distinguishing this from the default hybrid arrangement

The standard hybrid option - roughly three days in the office and two away, or fully
remote - is described in [remote and hybrid work](/workplace/remote-and-hybrid-work.md) and
does **not** require this procedure. This procedure is for a formal, individual variation
to hours, days or location beyond that default.

The two policies overlap where an employee wants a permanent location change, and the
handbook does not say which route governs. A permanent part-time transition following
parental leave is a third, separate route - see
[ramp-back time](/leave/ramp-back.md), which requires manager approval for the ongoing
arrangement.

# Gaps in the source

The handbook does not state:

* Any **grounds** on which a request may be refused.
* Any **appeal** route against a refusal.
* Whether a decision, once made, is **binding** or reviewable.
* Any limit on **how often** an employee may request.

Two months is a long decision window with no stated interim position, and the source does
not say what applies while a request is pending.
