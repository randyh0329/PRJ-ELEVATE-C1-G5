# Scope

Applies to all employees, temps, vendors and contractors, for business courtesies -
gifts, entertainment, travel, meals - involving **current or prospective non-government**
customers, suppliers and partners. Non-compliance may result in discipline up to and
including termination of employment or working arrangement.[^hb-14]

For government officials, use
[anti-bribery and government ethics](/ethics/anti-bribery.md) instead - the thresholds
there are far lower.[^hb-14]

**Core policy:** do not bribe, and do not give or accept business courtesies that may
create the **appearance** of a bribe. A bribe is giving money, gifts or something of value
to improperly influence the recipient's actions or business decisions.[^hb-14]

# Prohibited outright

Employees may not give or accept business courtesies involving:[^hb-14]

* **Illegal activity** of any kind.
* **Gambling** with customers, suppliers or business partners - **irrespective of whether
  participants use their own money or company funds**.
* **Adult entertainment** - including but not limited to strip clubs, hostess bars, room
  salons, and venues where one can pay for sexual activity.
* **Cash**, strictly prohibited; and **cash equivalents** - gift certificates, gift cards,
  virtual money - prohibited except under limited circumstances allowed in the Gift Policy
  FAQs.
* **Onsite TVCs:** employees are prohibited from giving swag or gifts, including cash, to
  onsite temporary staff, vendors and contractors.

# Frequency limit

As a general principle, do not give or accept business courtesies involving **the same
customer or business partner more than twice in any 3-month period**.[^hb-14]

**Exception for joint entertainment.** Where both the employee and the partner are present
- meals, travel - the frequency may exceed twice in 3 months **as long as it is moderate
and creates no appearance of impropriety**.[^hb-14]

# Decision factors

Before giving or accepting, evaluate:[^hb-14]

1. Whether there is a legitimate business purpose
2. The status of any current negotiations
3. The total market value and reasonableness of the courtesy
4. Potential conflicts of interest
5. **Whether the recipient is permitted to accept it under their own employer's policies**

# Pre-approval tiers

Business courtesies over **US$100 per person** require written pre-approval, because they
may create an appearance of impropriety.[^hb-14]

| Value per person | Written pre-approval from |
|------------------|---------------------------|
| Under US$100 | **None required** |
| Over US$100, up to US$250 | **Manager** |
| Over US$250, up to US$500 | **Director** (or country manager if there is no in-country director) |
| Over US$500 | **VP** |

# Purchasing power

Employees able to **influence company spending** with a particular vendor or supplier must
exercise **particular caution**. Never accept a business courtesy if it would adversely
affect your ability to decide in Altostrat's best interest, **or create the appearance that
it could**.[^hb-14]

# Conflict: the tier boundaries

| Source | Boundaries |
|--------|-----------|
| Section 14.4[^hb-14] | "Over US$100 **up to** US$250" → Manager; "Over US$250 **up to** US$500" → Director; "**Over** US$500" → VP. Unambiguous |
| Section 5.2[^hb-5-2] | "US $100 **to** US $250" → Manager; "US $250 **to** US $500" → Director; "**Over** US $500" → VP. Both ends inclusive, so **US$250 falls in two tiers and US$500 falls in two tiers** |

At exactly US$250 the summary layer permits either Manager or Director approval; at exactly
US$500, either Director or VP. The detail layer governs under the bundle's precedence
convention, so **US$250 → Manager** and **US$500 → Director**. A request landing exactly on
a boundary should still be confirmed with Ethics rather than answered from this corpus.
Logged in the [source defect register](/references/source-defects.md).
